## Exercise 9.1: Sweep the hint's authority *(warm-up)*

**Goal.** The lab plants the hint as "a colleague suggests…" and finds flip rate 0.03 (1/31, silent). But 15/31 answers *moved* somewhere else: the hint destabilizes more than it persuades. Test whether authority changes that balance.

**Steps.**
1. Find `HINT_TEMPLATE` and run the hint stage three times with different personas: "a colleague suggests", "your professor is certain", "a random guess someone made is".
2. For each, record flip rate, acknowledgment rate, silent-flip rate, and the distractor count (answers that changed but not to the hint).
3. Keep n=32 and greedy decoding so the runs are comparable.

**Hint.** Only the template string changes; the whole battery downstream (including `bootstrap_ci`) is template-agnostic. Budget ~30 s per persona on MPS fp16 (the ledger's hint stage cost 29.5 s).

**Solution sketch.** A loop over three template strings around the existing hint-stage cells. The colleague anchors are flip 0.03 [0.00, 0.10] and ack 0.10; the other personas are unledgered; record what you find and compare against the chapter's numbers. Report the distractor count alongside flip rate every time: the ledger shows flip rate alone would miss most of the hint's effect at 0.5B.

## Exercise 9.2: A finer truncation grid *(core)*

**Goal.** The early-answering curve is sampled at 0/25/50/75/100% of the CoT and jumps from 0.10 to 0.97 between 75% and 100%. Where in the last quarter does the answer actually get fixed?

**Steps.**
1. In the truncation cell, extend the fraction list to `[0.75, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95, 1.0]`.
2. Re-run the forced-early-answer stage (8-token budget) at each fraction; compute `early_answer_consistency` with bootstrap CIs.
3. Plot the refined curve; overlay the ledger's coarse points (0.06 / 0.00 / 0.00 / 0.10 / 0.97).

**Hint.** Truncation is by token count of the stored CoT; reuse the existing slicing helper. The ledger's whole truncation stage was 7.2 s for 155 forced completions, so a finer grid stays cheap.

**Solution sketch.** Change one list, rerun, plot. The fine-grained shape is unledgered. Record what you find. Interpretation guide: a curve hugging the floor until ~95% means the final answer is computed in the last arithmetic step (consistent with the ledger's "computes as it writes" reading); an earlier ramp would mean the conclusion crystallizes before the final line, i.e., some trailing text is narration after all, a more Lanham-like result than the coarse grid could see.

## Exercise 9.3: Where you inject the mistake should matter *(stretch)*

**Goal.** Mistake-injection sensitivity is 0.83 when the *middle* number of the CoT is corrupted (+3). If the model truly computes sequentially, corrupting the *first* number should propagate more, and corrupting the *last* should be most reliably caught by the final answer. Test it.

**Steps.**
1. In the mistake-injection cell, parameterize which number gets `+3`: first / middle / last numeric token of the CoT prefix.
2. Run all three conditions on the same 32 items, greedy, and compute `mistake_injection_sensitivity` with CIs for each.
3. Read 3 transcripts per condition: does the model *repair* early mistakes (recomputing correctly downstream) or *propagate* them?

**Hint.** Repair vs propagation is the mechanistically interesting split: sensitivity counts changed final answers, so a *repaired* early mistake shows up as LOW sensitivity; read transcripts before concluding "the model ignored the corruption." Also watch the parse-failure count (the ledger scored 30 of 31 with 1 unparseable continuation; your conditions may differ).

**Solution sketch.** Make the injection index an argument of the corruption helper, loop three values, tabulate. Middle-position anchor: 0.83 [0.68, 0.96]. First/last are unledgered. Record what you find and compare against the chapter's number. Whatever the ordering, you now have a positional sensitivity profile, a sharper instrument than the single-number battery, and a natural companion to Chapter 12, which retrains against exactly these metrics.
