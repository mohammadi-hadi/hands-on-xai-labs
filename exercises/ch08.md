## Exercise 8.1: Verify "Thirty tweets buys you a floor, not a leaderboard" *(warm-up)*

**Goal.** At n=30, all three methods separate from random on deletion-AUC (0.495/0.526/0.543 vs 0.678, CIs disjoint) but no method separates from another. Measure how the CIs behave as n shrinks, and convince yourself why the chapter refuses to crown LIME.

**Steps.**
1. In the bootstrap cell, subsample the 30 tweets to n ∈ {10, 20, 30} (seeded), recomputing deletion-AUC CIs for LIME, SHAP, IG, and random at each n.
2. For each n, print two booleans: "all methods separate from random?" and "any method separates from another method?"
3. Plot CI width vs n.

**Hint.** No new model calls needed: the per-tweet AUCs are already computed; you are only re-bootstrapping subsets of them. 2,000 resamples is cheap.

**Solution sketch.** ~10 lines: slice the per-tweet AUC arrays, rerun the percentile bootstrap. At n=30 you should see roughly the ledger's picture (random separates; methods overlap). At n=10, record whether even the random floor separation survives. The exercise output is the sample-size sentence you should carry into any explanation bake-off you run at work.

## Exercise 8.2: Chance-level plausibility depends on k and length *(core)*

**Goal.** The ledger's quiet warning: the *random* explainer scores plausibility token-F1 0.17 against human rationales, well above zero, because short tweets with 2–3 rationale words give random top-k picks real overlap. Map that chance floor so you can read plausibility scores correctly.

**Steps.**
1. In the plausibility cell (token-F1 vs the 8 human-annotated rationales), vary the k used for the top-k explanation words: k ∈ {1, 3, 5, 8}.
2. For each k, compute F1 for LIME/SHAP/IG *and* for 50 seeded random rankings (report the random mean).
3. Plot method F1 minus random-mean F1 against k: the honest, floor-adjusted plausibility.

**Hint.** The random floor rises mechanically with k (more picks, more chance overlap) while the number of gold rationale words stays fixed, so raw F1 comparisons across k values are meaningless without the subtraction.

**Solution sketch.** Two nested loops over k and seeds, reusing the existing F1 helper. Anchors at k=5: LIME 0.52 / SHAP 0.50 / IG 0.46 vs random 0.17. Other k values are unledgered. Record what you find and compare against the chapter's numbers. Deliverable: one sentence stating at which k the methods' *margin over chance* is widest, which is the number that should headline any plausibility claim.

## Exercise 8.3: Give the judge a fighting chance: swap-and-aggregate *(stretch)*

**Goal.** The 0.5B judge followed the slot on 16/16 pairs (32/32 verdicts = "A"). The standard mitigation in the literature the chapter cites is order-swapping with aggregation: keep only verdicts consistent across both orders, mark the rest as abstentions. Implement it, and measure what it costs in coverage.

**Steps.**
1. Reuse the exercise cell's `my_verdict` harness; for each pair, collect both orderings (already done) and define the aggregated verdict: the common choice if consistent, else `abstain`.
2. Report three numbers: coverage (non-abstain fraction), accuracy-on-covered (vs the known good/bad construction), and the raw position-bias rate.
3. Repeat with one prompt hardening: append "The correct choice is equally likely to be A or B." Does anything move?
4. If you have a GPU, run the same protocol on `Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` and compare.

**Hint.** With a 100% slot-follower, swap-and-aggregate yields *zero* coverage: the mitigation converts a silently wrong instrument into an honestly useless one. That distinction is the point.

**Solution sketch.** ~10 lines over the existing pair loop. With the book's 0.5B judge you should see roughly coverage 0/16 (every pair inconsistent under swap; the ledger's 16/16 position-following, seen from the other side). Prompt-hardening and larger-judge results are unledgered; record what you find. The deliverable is the chapter's decision rule made concrete: a judge is usable only when swap-consistent coverage is high AND accuracy-on-covered beats chance; report all three numbers or none.
