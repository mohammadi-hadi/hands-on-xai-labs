## Exercise 11.1: How fragile is the log-prob channel to wording? *(warm-up)*

**Goal.** The log-prob score is log P(" always justifiable.") − log P(" never justifiable.") after the prefix "In my view, {behavior} is". The chapter's claims discipline demands knowing how much of gpt2's headline r = 0.61 (Netherlands) survives a paraphrase.

**Steps.**
1. In the `logprob_delta` helper, add two prefix variants: "Most people would say {behavior} is" and "{behavior} is", keeping the same completion pair.
2. Rerun the 19-item scoring for all three models under each variant (the full run is 17.3 s on CPU, so three variants stay trivial).
3. Rebuild the per-country Pearson-r table per variant and compare against the committed table (gpt2 NL 0.61, Iran −0.22; Qwen NL 0.30; SmolLM2 NL −0.19).

**Hint.** "In my view" vs "Most people would say" is a first-person/descriptive-norm switch: if the model distinguishes them, the r profile may shift systematically, not just noisily.

**Solution sketch.** A loop over three prefix strings around the existing scoring loop. Variant results are unledgered. Record what you find and compare against the chapter's table. Read them against the ledger's uncertainty note: with 19 items, bootstrap CI widths had median 0.83, so treat any variant-to-variant change smaller than that as within-noise, and only wording effects that flip the W.E.I.R.D.-gap *sign* as findings.

## Exercise 11.2: Is "7 for everything" decoding-deep or distribution-deep? *(core)*

**Goal.** Qwen answered "7" for 13/19 items under greedy decoding, and SmolLM2 answered "1" for all 19. Test whether the generative channel's collapse is a property of greedy decoding or of the underlying distribution.

**Steps.**
1. In `generative_score`, add a sampled mode: `do_sample=True, temperature=0.7`, 10 samples per item (seeded).
2. For each model × item, record the sample distribution of parsed 1–10 answers; compare the per-item sample *mean* to the greedy answer.
3. Recompute the elicitation-agreement check: Spearman ρ between the sampled-mean scores and the log-prob deltas (Qwen's greedy-vs-log-prob was −0.18).

**Hint.** Keep the parse-failure guard, since sampling produces more unparseable outputs than greedy; report the compliance rate per model alongside (greedy anchors: Qwen 1.00, SmolLM2 1.00, gpt2 0.16).

**Solution sketch.** ~10 lines: a sampling loop inside the scoring pass, then the existing correlation code on sample means. Unledgered; record what you find. Interpretation grid: if sampled means still cluster on one value, the flattening lives in the trained distribution (the ledger reads SmolLM2's constant "1" as safety tuning); if sampling recovers item-to-item variation that correlates with the log-prob channel, the two "microphones" disagreed mostly about decoding, and the chapter's dual-measurement argument gets a mechanistic footnote.

## Exercise 11.3: Power analysis on your own audit *(stretch)*

**Goal.** The ledger's sample-size lesson: with 19 items, most CIs cross zero (median width 0.83, max 1.28), so "W.E.I.R.D. vs not" is defensible but "more Dutch than German" is not. Make that concrete by measuring CI width as a function of item count.

**Steps.**
1. Reuse the bootstrap machinery: for n ∈ {6, 10, 15, 19}, resample n items (without replacement, 50 seeded draws per n) and compute the bootstrap CI width of gpt2-vs-Netherlands r at each n.
2. Plot median CI width vs n; extrapolate by eye how many items a width of 0.3 would need.
3. Cross-check with the smoke-mode configuration (6 items). This is why smoke-mode correlations in this chapter are illustrative only.

**Hint.** CI width for a correlation scales roughly like 1/sqrt(n), so your empirical curve should bend accordingly, and the extrapolation to width 0.3 lands far above what 19 survey items can provide.

**Solution sketch.** Two nested loops over n and draws, reusing `pearson_nan` and the percentile bootstrap. Anchors: median width 0.83 at n=19. The n-curve is unledgered. Record what you find. Deliverable: a one-line audit-design rule ("to claim X at CI width Y, I need ~Z items"). That is the difference between an audit and an anecdote, and the reason the WVS module of the real study uses far more than 19 items per construct.
