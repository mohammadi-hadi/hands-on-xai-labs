## Exercise 2.1: Put a number on the disagreement *(warm-up)*

**Goal.** The heatmap (`fig-five-explanations`) shows disagreement visually. Quantify it with rank correlations, and compare against what Chapter 4 later measures on its own specimen (Kendall τ between methods of 0.43–0.59).

**Steps.**
1. After the cell that builds `rows` (the 4×n score matrix), add a cell computing pairwise Kendall τ between `lime_scores`, `shap_scores`, and `ig_scores` (`scipy.stats.kendalltau`).
2. For attention, correlate against the *absolute values* of the signed methods, since attention has no sign, so a signed comparison is unfair by construction.
3. Print the 3×3 (signed) and the attention-vs-|method| values.

**Hint.** Use the raw score vectors, not the per-method-normalized `rows`. Normalization does not change ranks, but it is cleaner to correlate the originals.

**Solution sketch.**
```python
from scipy.stats import kendalltau
for a, b in [("lime", "shap"), ("lime", "ig"), ("shap", "ig")]:
    print(a, b, kendalltau(scores[a], scores[b]).statistic)
```
One sentence is an anecdote, not a distribution: your τ values will bounce around. Record them and compare against Chapter 4's τ = 0.43–0.59 measured over 10 tweets on a different model. The qualitative point (respected methods only partially agree on the identical prediction) should survive.

## Exercise 2.2: How much of the disagreement is just sampling noise? *(core)*

**Goal.** LIME and SHAP are stochastic samplers (the notebook uses `num_samples=800` and `max_evals=400` in full mode, 60/50 in smoke). Before blaming "the methods," measure how much a method disagrees *with itself* across seeds.

**Steps.**
1. In the LIME cell, wrap the `explain_instance` call in a loop over 5 seeds (`LimeTextExplainer(..., random_state=s)` for `s in range(5)`), at `num_samples=60` and again at 800.
2. Compute the mean pairwise Kendall τ among the 5 runs at each budget.
3. Do the same for SHAP with `max_evals=50` vs 400 (note: partition SHAP is far less seed-sensitive than LIME; say why in one sentence).
4. Compare within-method τ to the between-method τ from Exercise 2.1.

**Hint.** If within-method τ at the small budget is comparable to between-method τ, part of "the methods disagree" is really "the estimates are noisy." Chapter 4's ledger measured exactly this pattern for LIME (seed-to-seed τ rising from 0.62 to 0.83 when going from 500 to 2000 samples).

**Solution sketch.** Loop, collect score vectors, `kendalltau` all pairs, print means per budget. Record what you find and compare against Chapter 4's stability numbers; the expected shape is within-method agreement rising with budget while between-method disagreement persists: the disagreement is real, not just noise.
