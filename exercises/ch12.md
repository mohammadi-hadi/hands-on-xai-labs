# Chapter 12 Exercises

**Built-in notebook exercise (closing cells of `ch12-training-for-explainability.ipynb`):** teach the model to *cite its evidence*: build DPO pairs whose chosen completion verifies with the inverse operation ("Check: 12 − 7 = 5"), retrain with `RETRAIN = True`, and measure with the criterion-specific `check_rate` metric (0.00 for base/SFT/DPO before your retrain, because nothing ever contrasted checking with not checking).

Committed numbers are in `experiments/ch12-results.md`. Big caveat before comparing: training here is device-dependent: the committed MPS run and a CPU fp32 run of the identical pipeline produced measurably different models (0/20 vs 5/20 acknowledged flips), same seed. Compare *patterns*, not digits, unless you match the ledger's device.

## Exercise 12.1: Persona transfer: does the disposition generalize? *(warm-up)*

**Goal.** The DPO model acknowledges the hint at rate 1.00. But training and evaluation both used the "colleague" persona. The notebook flags this as in-distribution by design. Measure whether acknowledgment survives unseen personas.

**Steps.**
1. Keep the trained DPO model from a full run.
2. Rebuild the hinted eval prompts with new personas never seen in training: "my manager", "an online calculator", "a note in the margin says".
3. Rerun `hint_battery` per persona; report acknowledgment, flip, and silent-flip rates with bootstrap CIs, next to the colleague-persona anchors (ack 1.00, silent flips 0.00).

**Hint.** Check what Chapter 9's acknowledgment regex actually matches before concluding "no transfer". If the model says "the manager's suggestion" and the regex only catches colleague-vocabulary, you have a metric miss, not a behavior miss. The ledger admits this circularity explicitly; read transcripts.

**Solution sketch.** A loop over persona strings around the existing prompt builder + battery. Unledgered; record what you find and compare against the chapter's in-distribution numbers. Both failure modes are informative: no transfer means DPO taught a template, not a disposition; regex-only failure means your measurement, like the chapter's, is part of the training loop.

## Exercise 12.2: Contrast accuracy and watch what moves *(core)*

**Goal.** The chapter's thesis in one table: DPO moved acknowledgment from 0.00 to 1.00 and silent flips to 0.00 while accuracy stayed pinned at 0.00 in all three columns, because chosen and rejected never disagreed about arithmetic. Build pairs that *do* contrast correctness and test whether preference optimization can buy accuracy from an 82M model.

**Steps.**
1. In the pair-builder cells, create a new pair set: chosen = correct worked line + correct answer; rejected = same format with a wrong result (e.g. gold+2), both acknowledging the hint identically so only correctness differs.
2. Retrain DPO from the same SFT checkpoint (150 steps, β=0.1, as shipped).
3. Rerun the full battery; compare accuracy, ack, and silent-flip columns to the committed table.

**Hint.** The ledger's stated reason accuracy stayed 0.00 is capability, not signal ("an 82M-parameter model simply can't compute it reliably"), so the hypothesis you are testing is that preference pressure cannot exceed capacity. Watch the DPO loss curve: pair-accuracy hitting 1.00 while eval accuracy stays 0.00 would be the cleanest demonstration that the model learned to *rank* correct-looking text without learning arithmetic.

**Solution sketch.** ~15 lines: new `comp_*` builders, reuse `dpo_loss` training loop, rerun battery. Unledgered. Record what you find and compare against the committed table (accuracy 0.00/0.00/0.00). Expected shape given the ledger's capability note: training metrics saturate, eval accuracy barely moves; "you get what you contrast" has a ceiling called "what the model can compute."

## Exercise 12.3: Walk into the over-optimization zone on purpose *(stretch)*

**Goal.** The committed run shows the DPO loss saturating (falling from 0.087 to 0.0009, pair-accuracy 1.00 from step 25) while the implied reward margin keeps climbing (from +2.6 to +7.4): the over-optimization zone, visible as mode collapse onto 1–2 of the 4 acknowledgment openers. Map how β and step count control entry into that zone.

**Steps.**
1. Run the shipped DPO training at β ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.5} × steps ∈ {75, 150, 300} (9 cheap runs; the full pipeline is ~30 s per run on MPS, somewhat more on CPU).
2. For each run, record: final margin, battery metrics, opener diversity (count distinct acknowledgment openers across the 20 eval generations), and one transcript.
3. Plot margin vs opener diversity across runs.

**Hint.** β scales how hard the model is pushed away from the SFT reference: small β = stronger effective pressure per nat of margin. The "verification theater" transcript in the ledger ("let me verify… 8 * 4 = −4") is the qualitative signature to look for as margins grow.

**Solution sketch.** Nested loop over (β, steps) re-running the ~15-line DPO function, collecting a 9-row table. Anchor: β=0.1/150 steps gives margin +7.4, heavy opener collapse. The rest of the grid is unledgered. Record what you find and compare against the chapter's single point. Deliverable: your own early-stopping rule stated in one line (e.g. "stop when pair-accuracy holds 1.00 for N steps"), which is precisely the knob the real GRPO adapters in the study were early-stopped on.
