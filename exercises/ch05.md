## Exercise 5.1: Ablate the *quietest* heads: the missing control *(warm-up)*

**Goal.** The lab shows that zeroing the loudest last-layer head barely moves anything (mean |Δp| 0.007) and even 6 loudest heads only shift p by ≤ 0.10 with 0/8 label flips. But "loudest" was never compared to "quietest." Add the control: does ablating the 6 *least*-attended heads do just as little?

**Steps.**
1. Find the head-ablation cell (it builds a `head_mask` and zeroes the top heads by [CLS]-attention mass).
2. Invert the selection: sort ascending, zero the bottom 6 heads of the final layer.
3. Report mean |Δp(hate)| and map correlation before/after, next to the ledger's loud-head numbers (0.062 mean, map ρ mean 0.92).

**Hint.** Only the `argsort` direction changes; everything downstream of `head_mask` is identical.

**Solution sketch.** Flip one `[::-1]`, rerun the loop, print two rows. The quiet-head result is not in the ledger; record what you find and compare against the chapter's numbers. If quiet and loud ablations cost about the same, the "loudness" ranking carries little causal information at this layer, a sharper version of the chapter's redundancy point. If loud heads cost measurably more, you've found the causal signal the raw maps were hinting at.

## Exercise 5.2: The full layer-by-layer aggregation-trap matrix *(core)*

**Goal.** The ledger's sharpest number: layer-6 mean vs layer-3 mean rank correlation is *negative* (−0.15; max-vs-mean cross-layer −0.21), while within-layer aggregations mostly agree (0.95). Extend the 3-strip comparison to the full 6×6 layer matrix and find where the story flips.

**Steps.**
1. In the aggregation cell (showcase tweet), compute the [CLS]-row head-mean attention over content tokens for *all* 6 layers, not just 3 and 6.
2. Build the 6×6 Spearman-ρ matrix across layers; render as a heatmap.
3. Mark the ledger's two committed cells (L6-vs-L3 ≈ −0.15) as a checksum, and locate the boundary layer where correlations with layer 6 turn positive.
4. Repeat for one more tweet to check it is not a showcase artifact.

**Hint.** You already have the full attention stack from one forward pass; this is pure numpy on cached tensors. Remember to drop [CLS]/[SEP] positions before correlating (the ledger measured 32% of last-layer [CLS] mass parked on specials).

**Solution sketch.** One loop building a `(6, n_words)` matrix, then `scipy.stats.spearmanr` pairwise. The L6/L3 cell should land near −0.15 with the committed specimen. The rest of the matrix is unledgered, so record what you find. Expected shape: a block structure where early layers (special-token-sink dominated; the ledger notes layer 3's [CLS] row is almost entirely [SEP]-gazing) anti-correlate with late layers. The practical rule it grounds: "which layer" is a bigger researcher degree of freedom than "mean or max."

## Exercise 5.3: Does discounting the sink fix the corroboration failure? *(stretch)*

**Goal.** Attention-vs-|IG| agreement on this specimen is ρ 0.01 ± 0.40 (raw), essentially zero on average. The ledger separately measures that 32% of [CLS] attention lands on special tokens. Test a hypothesis the chapter raises via its "special-token discount" checklist item but never tests: does *removing and renormalizing* the special-token mass improve corroboration with IG?

**Steps.**
1. In the attention-vs-IG cell (8 tweets), before correlating, zero the [CLS]/[SEP] positions in the attention vector and renormalize to sum 1.
2. Recompute per-tweet Spearman ρ vs |IG| for raw last-layer and rollout attention.
3. Compare the new mean ± std and per-tweet range against the ledger's 0.01 ± 0.40 (raw) and −0.01 ± 0.42 (rollout).

**Hint.** IG word scores already exclude specials (sub-words summed to words), so the mismatch you are removing is purely on the attention side. Keep the per-tweet scatter: the ledger warns the mean alone misleads (range −0.60 to 0.82).

**Solution sketch.** Three lines inside the existing loop: mask, renormalize, correlate. Not in the ledger. Record what you find and compare against the chapter's numbers. Both outcomes teach: if ρ stays ≈ 0, the corroboration failure is not a bookkeeping artifact of sink mass, strengthening the chapter's verdict; if ρ improves materially, you've quantified exactly how much of "attention is unreliable" was really "nobody subtracted the resting position." Either way, update your own five-line scorecard.
